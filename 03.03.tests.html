All cards at a glance

LessonFrontBack
1What are the five stages of the attacker's playbook, in order?Reconnaissance → scanning → gaining access → maintaining access → covering tracks.
1What is the difference between reconnaissance and scanning?Reconnaissance reads public information without touching the target's systems; scanning sends packets to the target to discover hosts, open ports and versions.
1What is the single idea behind the Cyber Kill Chain?The attacker must complete every step; a defence that stops any one step stops the whole attack.
1In MITRE ATT&CK, what is a tactic and what is a technique?A tactic is the attacker's goal (a column, e.g. persistence); a technique is a concrete method to reach it (a cell, e.g. T1566 phishing).
1Why is a gap in a log file a signal?Covering tracks deletes evidence; a log that stops and restarts shows that someone removed entries — the absence is the evidence.
2What is OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)?Open-source intelligence: collecting and analysing information from publicly available sources without touching the target's systems.
2What is a pretext?The invented but consistent story built from reconnaissance that the attacker tells to obtain an action or a secret.
2Phishing, smishing, vishing, spear phishing — what is the difference?Same technique, different channel: e-mail, SMS (Short Message Service), voice call; spear phishing is phishing written for one specific target.
2Name the four verbs of the correct response to a suspected phishing message.Do not act · verify through a channel you already trust · report to IT (Information Technology) · preserve the message.
2What do SPF (Sender Policy Framework), DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) and DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) check?SPF (Sender Policy Framework): is the sending server allowed for this domain? DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail): is the message signed by the domain? DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance): what to do when SPF (Sender Policy Framework)/DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) fail (none, quarantine, reject).
3Formula for the keyspace?N^L — alphabet size N (sum of the character classes present: 26 + 26 + 10 + 33) to the power of the length L.
3Formula for the entropy in bits?L × log₂ N (log₂ 26 ≈ 4.70, log₂ 36 ≈ 5.17, log₂ 62 ≈ 5.95, log₂ 95 ≈ 6.57).
3Formula for the worst-case cracking time?Keyspace ÷ guess rate (N^L ÷ rate); the average case is half of it.
3Why does a dictionary attack beat the arithmetic?The arithmetic assumes every combination is equally likely; real passwords are words plus rules, so the attacker tries the likely ones first and finds Hiking2026 in minutes, not years.
3What is credential stuffing?Trying username/password pairs leaked from one site on other sites; it works because people reuse passwords — the fix is unique passwords (manager) plus MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication).
4SQL (Structured Query Language) injection — one line?Hostile input (admin'--) changes the meaning of a database query built from user input; breaks confidentiality and integrity.
4Cross-site scripting — one line?A script stored or reflected by the application runs in other users' browsers, typically to steal session cookies.
4Session hijacking — one line?A copied session identifier lets the attacker act as a logged-in user without any password.
4Man-in-the-middle (on-path) — one line?The attacker inserts their machine between two parties (forged ARP (Address Resolution Protocol) on a shared Wi-Fi) and reads or alters unencrypted traffic.
4Denial of service — one line?Capacity is exhausted (SYN flood of half-open connections; distributed when a botnet is used); breaks availability.
5Name the three classification axes.Target (network / host / application / human), effect (C / I / A), actor (script kiddie / insider / hacktivist / organized crime / nation-state).
5What distinguishes an insider from the other actors?Legitimate access and knowledge of the processes; countered by least privilege, separation of duties and leaver procedures.
5What does APT (Advanced Persistent Threat) stand for and which actor does it describe?Advanced Persistent Threat — the long, patient, well-resourced intrusions typical of nation-state groups.
5White, grey, black hat — in one line each.White: authorized in writing and reported. Grey: unauthorized, no criminal intent. Black: unauthorized and criminal.
5What does the law look at — intent or authorization?Authorization. Under Criminal Code s. 342.1 (and the CFAA (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act), EU (European Union) Directive 2013/40/EU, Spain's LO (Learning Outcome) 10/1995) unauthorized access is an offence regardless of intent.
